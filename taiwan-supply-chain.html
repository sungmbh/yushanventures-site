How the work runs

The tier that will quote you is not the tier you have heard of.

Most supply-chain advice stops at the country, which is useless the moment you have a bill of materials. The ODMs everyone can name are built for volume: a first run of fifty units does not clear their minimum, and the enquiry dies in a mailbox. Underneath them sits a far deeper bench — hundreds of houses with the same equipment and no marketing department — who will quote that run if someone qualifies them, translates the spec, and vouches for the customer. That introduction is what we offer.

What we offer

  • Bill-of-materials review against real Taiwan capability — where the island is deep, where it is thin, and where a part has to come from somewhere else entirely
  • Supplier qualification on the ground: capability, tolerances, quoting behaviour, and how a house handles a first-time foreign customer
  • Routing your BOM to the two or three partners who can hold it, with the spec translated and expectations set on both sides
  • Foundry and design-house strategy for silicon — dual-foundry questions, node selection, and where capacity is actually constrained
  • Support through quoting, first article and ramp, including the cross-cultural preparation Korean, Japanese and Taiwanese counterparts require

Who this is for

  • Foreign deep-tech startups with a funded round and a hardware roadmap that has reached Taiwan
  • AI silicon and accelerators, optics and photonics, sensing, power electronics, advanced materials
  • Corporates standing up a second supply base outside China

What you get

  • A layer-by-layer read on your own BOM, with the constrained items flagged before they become a schedule problem
  • Named, qualified counterparts — not a directory — and the meetings to go with them
